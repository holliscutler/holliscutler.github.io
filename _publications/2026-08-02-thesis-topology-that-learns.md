The thesis builds on the design principle set out in the
[ALIFE 2026 paper](/publication/2026-08-01-alife-topology-that-learns):
biological flow networks such as fungal mycelium and *Physarum polycephalum*
store memory in their own structure, strengthening pathways that carry flow and
letting unused ones decay. Treated as a forecasting mechanism, that yields a
predictor whose topology adapts with the data it processes, instead of one with
fixed structure and learned weights.

Where the paper establishes the mechanism, the thesis takes it to **categorical
drift** — the setting where the regime generating a stream changes, not merely
its parameters. That is the case fixed-structure online predictors handle worst,
and the one where a topology that reorganizes itself has the most to offer.
